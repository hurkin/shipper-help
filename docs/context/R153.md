# Estimated Shrink

Estimates how much product was lost on a booking: the weight taken out of inventory
compared with the weight actually shipped.

## What's included

- Run for a booking, or for a SKU (every booking that used it).
- Inventory on the booking's cargo, on work orders on the booking, and on work orders
  on the booking's cargo. Only rows with a location count.
- For each SKU: beginning inventory (before the booking's first transaction), inbound
  loads received while the booking was being worked, each work order and cargo, the
  shrink, and ending inventory.

## How shrink is worked out

Shrink is the difference between the weight taken out of inventory and the weight
shipped:

> Shrink % = 1 − (total outbound ÷ total inbound)
>
> For example, 1 − (116,040 ÷ 118,429) = 0.02 = 2.0%.

- **Inbound weight** is the inventory used, priced at its unit weight: a tag uses the
  weight of the load it came in on; inventory without a tag uses the SKU's average.
- **Outbound weight** is each cargo's scale ticket net weight. **A starred cargo
  carried several SKUs,** so its weight is split by each SKU's share of the
  inventory weight.

## Supported ways of working

- A cargo that takes raw inventory out, produces processed inventory and takes the
  processed out (for example −56 raw, +20 processed, −20 processed).
- A cargo that takes processed inventory out of a location (−20 processed).
- A work order that takes raw out and produces processed (−56 raw, +20 processed).
- Inbound loads received while the booking is being processed.

## Reading the result

- **Inaccurate unit counts** always give inaccurate average raw weights, and so
  overstate or understate product afterwards. Product is weighed by the truckload
  but taken out by unit count, so counts must be accurate.
- **More than one SKU in a cargo:** the average raw weight comes from all earlier
  processed inventory, because first-in or last-in can't be assumed.
- **Back-stocked product** keeps its SKU but loses the link to its inbound scale
  ticket, so the average processed unit weight is used. This is a problem when unit
  weights vary widely between loads.
- **Negative shrink** usually means raw inventory is understated: more weight shipped
  than was used.
- **Shrink too high** usually means raw inventory is overstated.
